# Shot card

A shot card is a few labelled lines about one beat of a story. Code builds the picture from the card, and
remembers the people in it, so a card is only these lines:

who: the names of those in the picture, separated by semicolons, as they are named in Cast. Write nobody if no one is there.
does: one sentence, at most 30 words: what they are doing right now, where their hands and eyes are. If nobody is there, the main thing seen.
where: the place, the time of day and the weather, with two or three things that stand in it. At most 25 words. Start with in, on, at or under. Write same if it has not changed.
light: finish the phrase "lit by ...": the source, its direction, its quality and colour. At most 15 words. Write same if it has not changed.
mood: the palette and the feeling, in three or four words.
new: one line for each individual in who that is not yet in Cast, in four parts separated by |
     name | what they are, with age and gender | build, face, hair, clothes | their voice: pitch, timbre, speed, accent

Rules
- Only what can be seen. Concrete things: materials, colours, surfaces.
- Keep the beat's own words for people, places and things exactly as written: slang, dialect, coarse and unusual words are never tidied or explained.
- An individual already in Cast is only named in who. Never describe them again: code does that, the same way every time.
- Someone the beat does not name is called by what they are: the old man, the girl in red.
- Say what is there. Never write "no" or "without".
- Only if the guide or a Steer names a kind of picture (an oil painting, a cartoon, a woodcut), add a line style: with it.

Example
who: Maggie; the dog
does: Maggie hauls a wet net over the rail, both fists white on the rope, eyes on the water.
where: on the deck of a rusted trawler at dawn, drizzle falling, gulls on the wheelhouse roof
light: low cold sun from the left, through sea mist
mood: salt grey, rust, tired
new: Maggie | a woman in her sixties, a trawler skipper | broad, weather-cut face, cropped white hair, yellow oilskin | low gravelly voice, slow, Hull accent
new: the dog | a grey three-legged lurcher | wet wiry coat, one torn ear |
