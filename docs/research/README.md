Two cautions run through all of it. Most numbers describe **one** twenty-one minute reference set by one artist at
166 BPM. And the best cutting algorithm found (Collins, 2001) is a composer's stylisation that nobody has checked
against measured records. Everything below is a design to be tuned by ear, by someone who can hear it.

## The diagnosis

**Every decision mindstream makes is independent of every other, so repetition is the only relation it has.**

- A chopped layer has one fixed rhythm (the same two to six sixteenths, always including the one) and a fixed
  A A' A B form. 47 to 55 of every 64 bars are exact repeats of an earlier bar; a mutated layer is a literal
  four-bar loop. The fixed form is the "three alike and a fill" scheme that the reference measurement tested and
  rejected, and that no jungle source describes.
- Layers hear neither the drums nor each other. Two layers at the same density land on exactly the same sixteenths.
- A slice's own character (how loud, how sharp, how tonal, where it came from in the bar) is measured and then not
  used. Slices play in the sound's own order, at one length, one pitch, forwards, uncut at the end.
- The sub draws a new unrelated rhythm for every bar and a new unrelated riff every sixteen. The hook is two
  appearances of a random shape and then a new random shape. Hook, sub and drums coincide no more than parts from
  different sessions would. Nothing reads elapsed bars, so every sixteen-bar stretch is statistically the same.
- The tune is out of tune: there is no pitch tracking, and each piece of film sound is assumed to sit a tritone
  above the root. A piece that is really on the root plays every hook note 600 cents out.

The statistics are right and the intent is missing. Intent is dependence: bar to bar, lane to lane, note to its
target, phrase to the phrase before.

## What carries legibility instead of repetition

From [04], in order of how much they buy:

1. **A spine.** Three cells nearly certain (1, 5, 13); everything else free. All surprise is weighed against it.
2. **Identity as odds, not as pattern.** Chances per slot held for a section; each bar a fresh deal.
3. **The group returns, the bar does not.** Four different bars whose group comes back half the same.
4. **One source, small palette.** A slice keeps its identity through any reordering; stacked processing loses it.
5. **A fixed accent shape and hard edges.** Loud on the spine, quiet ghosts on odd steps, tails cut.
6. **Displacement by rule.** Move, rotate, repeat and truncate what is there; uneven blocks (3+3+2) say where you are.
7. **Nested clocks.** Each lane changes on its own slow period; rarely two at once.
8. **The low end owns time.** Chopped material stays out of the sub's register.
9. **A recurring gesture.** Stated, back exactly four bars later, perhaps once more, then retired.
10. **Relations between lanes.** One lane's events predict another's.
11. **Phrase-end as a category.** Something happens at some four-bar lines; what it is varies.
12. **Direction.** A change that can be extrapolated: a window walking through a sound, a filter opening.

And the rule for the edge ([04] section 5): **one dial past the lip at a time**, for one or two bars, returning on a
four-bar line with the spine and the low end. A state held eight bars earns a bigger disturbance than one held two.

## What to build, in order

Each step can be measured on the plan alone, without sound, against the reference's numbers.

**0. Tune the tune.** Track the pitch of each piece of film sound and repitch from what it is. Without this nothing
melodic can be judged. *Check: hook notes within 30 cents.*

**1. A chop engine in place of the four-bar plan.** This is the answer to "legible because it's repetitive".
- Give every source a role from what is already measured about it: anchor, carrier, colour, hook, stab, bed. One
  anchor and one carrier at a time.
- Cut in blocks: eighth-note units, blocks of one or three units (five rarely), each played once or twice, a new
  block reading the source at the current position so material lands on the step it came from. Shape the phrase:
  long blocks early, short ones and repeats late; a one-unit stutter closing a phrase one time in five.
- Constrain it: the heaviest slice class on step 1 in three bars of four, the sharpest on 5 always and on 13, 15 or
  11; ghosts kept with the hit they lead to; slices at their own loudness, the phrase's first downbeat loudest.
- Leave the cells that belong to another lane: off the one when the kick and sub have it, off the snare, thinner
  where the anchor speaks. Two layers never take the same cells.
- Operators, rationed per four-bar group (at most one roll, one reversal, one empty downbeat; at least one plain
  bar): roll with a rising level into the bar line, a reversed slice into the one, the last snare pitched up, a
  reset beat ("kickback"), a half-time bar, a drop-out at bar 8.
- Four different bars; the group's skeleton held for a section and re-dealt inside.
- Gate every slice at its slot with a short fade; skip leading silence.
- *Check: lag-1 similarity at the same-section baseline, lag 4 about 0.13 above it, exact repeats near zero, spine
  cells above 0.9, no two layers above 0.5 overlap.*

**2. A bass that is written.** A stored riff: a one-bar rhythm cell with two anchors (step 1, and 7 by default) and
ruled decorations; bar 2 derived from bar 1; a four-bar phrase that thins and turns round; a pitch skeleton with
targets, three home pitches and one colour note that always resolves; slides on small moves. Its relation to the
drums set per scene. The saw bass given a job of its own (answering in the sub's rests) or switched off.
*Check: agreement at 2 and 4 bars over 50% with lag 1 low; under 4% of onsets on step 6; every colour note resolved.*
*Built: `mindstream/bassline.py`, played by groove.py; `python -B demo/bass_check.py`.*

**3. Hooks that belong to the bass.** A motif with one distinctive interval, from a pitch set shared with the bass;
stated, answered four bars later with its ending changed, then fragmented, then replaced by a relative. Placed in
the sub's gaps, late in the bar, in bars 4, 5 and 8. *Check: hook onsets coincide with bass onsets less than chance.*
*Built: `mindstream/motif.py`, played by the lead in groove.py; `python -B demo/bass_check.py`.*

**4. Development.** One kept mutation per eight bars; at the end of a run the riff's fate drawn as measured (back
after a hole 36%, partly rewritten 21%, new 39%); a B riff at about 32 bars with the kick style and carrier
changing with it; A back, changed, at 64. A slice layer's source, role, odds and processing each on its own clock.
*Check: after 64 bars something heard earlier has come back changed.*
*Built: `mindstream/develop.py`, wired by `mindstream/course.py` into the riff, the hook, the drums and the chop;
`python -B demo/develop_check.py`.*

**5. Dials for the edge.** The parameters above gathered under two controls (locked to restless; composed to
deranged), with the one-dial-past-the-lip budget enforced by the program.
*Built: `mindstream/edge.py` (`!edge`, `!roller`, the knob panel's EDGE row), applied through `mindstream/course.py`;
`python -B demo/develop_check.py`.*

**6. The program measures itself.** The checks above run on the plan as it plays, so a session that has drifted
into a loop, or into noise, can be seen.
*Built: `mindstream/watch.py`, fed by the mixer a bar at a time, shown as HEALTH on the knob panel;
`python -B demo/watch_check.py`.*

## Choices for the owner

- **Which chop.** The research gives presets for jungle 1991-95, atmospheric, drumfunk, techstep, drill and bass,
  breakcore, footwork and the revival. They differ in cut unit, how much source order survives, how many repeats,
  and whether rolls are allowed outside phrase ends. Which should be the default, and should it be a control?
- **Roller or switch-up.** How long a riff is held before it changes is a dial from 32 bars to 2.
- **Where the session starts.** It still opens in four-on-the-floor at 120 BPM.
